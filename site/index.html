Satellite batteries

Small satellites, heard by volunteer radio stations around the world. A battery should charge in sunlight and sag in the Earth's shadow, so an alert here only counts if the satellite was in sunlight at the time.

How it works

1
Hear

Volunteer ground stations record each satellite's radio beacons and share them through SatNOGS.

2
Decode

The raw bytes of every beacon become a battery voltage, and a current where the satellite reports it.

3
Compare with the Sun

An orbit model says whether the satellite was in sunlight or in the Earth's shadow when it sent each beacon, and the rules only judge what the light allows.

What this is not. A learning project, not an operations tool. It refreshes every hour, not live. The rules were checked against a few days of real data and have not yet been shown to catch a real fault. Some voltage units are taken from the satellite's published format or assumed from the size of the numbers.

Code and the full story: satnogs-pipeline, with the orbit model of orbitscreen, whose live tracker shows these satellites too. Beacons from SatNOGS, orbit data from SatNOGS DB and CelesTrak.
