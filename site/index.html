Satellite batteries

Small satellites, heard by volunteer radio stations around the world. A battery is supposed to discharge while its satellite is in the Earth's shadow, so an alert here only counts if the satellite was in sunlight at the time.

How to read this. Each dot is one beacon a satellite sent. The shaded stretches are when the satellite was in the Earth's shadow, worked out from its orbit and the Sun's position (not from the battery). A healthy battery stays up in the light and sags in the shadow.

What this is not. A learning project, not an operations tool. It is refreshed every hour, not live. The rules were checked against a few days of real data and have not yet been shown to catch a real fault. Some voltage units are taken from the satellite's published format or assumed from the size of the numbers.

Code and the full story: satnogs-pipeline, which uses the orbit model of orbitscreen. Beacons from SatNOGS, orbit data from CelesTrak.
